How free, “audit” and certificate courses work
  • Free: Khan Academy, MIT OpenCourseWare (no sign-up needed), HHMI BioInteractive, iBiology, LabXchange and Rosalind are free to use.
  • Free to audit: on Coursera you can usually watch lectures and read the materials for free. Graded assignments and the certificate cost money. If you do not see an audit button, look for a free trial or apply for financial aid on the course page.
  • Certificates are optional. What matters most is being able to explain what you learned and show something you made with it, like a project, a poster or a short write-up.
  • Credentials are different from courses. BACE, CITI training and the OSHA 10-hour card each have their own sign-up rules. For OSHA, only authorized providers can issue the real card.
  • Age rules: Coursera requires learners to be 13 or older, and under 18 a parent or guardian must be involved. Each card shows a suggested minimum age, and a site’s own rules always come first.
Suggested learning paths by level

These are starting points, not a required order. Pick one or two at a time, since finishing a course beats starting five.

Middle school (ages 10–13)Khan Academy Biology, then Khan Academy Statistics & Probability, then iBiology talks to hear how scientists work.
High school (ages 14–18)HHMI BioInteractive and LabXchange virtual labs to practice lab techniques, then Statistics & Probability, then Python for Everybody and Rosalind for bioinformatics. When you are ready, try MIT’s Fundamentals of Biology, and look at the BACE credential and the OSHA 10-hour card.
College (18+)Duke’s Introduction to Genetics and Evolution, UC San Diego’s Drug Discovery, then Python and Rosalind. Pair them with the NSF REU Directory and Pathways to Science for research, and CITI training for lab ethics.

Curious what a lab technician’s job looks like? The BLS Career Guide card has the education, work settings and outlook, and BioNJ lists New Jersey biotech employers.

Show your learning on your resume and LinkedIn
  • Add finished courses to your resume as coursework or training, with the school or platform and the skills you practiced. Build it in the Resume Builder.
  • If you earned a real certificate or credential, list it on LinkedIn under Licenses & Certifications. Otherwise mention the course in your Education or Projects description. The LinkedIn guide walks through it.
  • Turn what you learned into something to show: a science fair poster from the Poster Lab, a short coding project, or a one-page summary of a virtual lab.
  • Save the cards you plan to do with the heart button. They show up in My Saved Opportunities and on your printable plan, where you can track your status.
Staying safe online
  • Use the links on this page, which go straight to each official site.
  • If you are under 18, set up accounts with a parent or guardian, and never share your address, phone number or school schedule in a course forum.
  • Never pay a stranger for a “guaranteed” certificate or internship. If something advertised as free asks for card details, stop and ask a parent or teacher.
